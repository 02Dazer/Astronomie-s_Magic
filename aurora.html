Aurora Borealis

What Causes the Aurora Borealis?

The Aurora Borealis occurs when charged particles from the solar wind interact with Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere...

Where Can You See the Aurora Borealis?

The Aurora Borealis is typically visible in high-latitude regions around the Arctic, including:

  • Alaska
  • Canada
  • Iceland
  • Greenland
  • The Faroe Islands
  • Scandinavia
  • Finland
  • Scotland
  • Russia

When Is the Best Time to See the Aurora Borealis?

Auroras are more frequent and intense during the peak of the 11-year sunspot cycle or during geomagnetic storms. The best viewing times are usually between September and April, when the nights are darker and the aurora is more active.

Types of Aurora Borealis

There are several types of auroras, including:

  • Discrete Aurora: Structured into distinct features with well-defined edges, such as arcs, rays, and coronas.
  • Diffuse Aurora: Appears relatively structureless with indistinct edges and amorphous forms.
  • Pulsating Aurora: Intensity variations over short timescales, typically with periods of 2-20 seconds.
  • STEVE (Strong Thermal Emission Velocity Enhancement): A rare type of aurora caused by a ribbon of hot plasma in the upper atmosphere.

Colors of the Aurora Borealis

The colors of the aurora depend on the altitude and energy of the particles:

  • Green: Oxygen emissions at altitudes of around 100-200 km.
  • Red: Oxygen emissions at higher altitudes.
  • Blue/Purple: Nitrogen emissions at lower altitudes.

The Aurora Borealis is a complex and fascinating phenomenon, and scientists continue to study it to better understand the underlying physical processes.
