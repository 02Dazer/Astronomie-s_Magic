Black Holes

A black hole is a region in space characterized by immense gravity that makes it capable of pulling everything around it, including light, which makes it invisible. The boundary of a black hole is known as the "event horizon" , the point beyond which nothing can escape.

How is a black hole formed?

  • A black hole forms when very massive stars collapse on themselves at the end of their life cycle, compressing their mass into a very small space.
  • There are also black holes believed to have formed since the Big Bang.
    •

    Types of black holes:

  • Primordial black holes: Extremely small, thought to have formed in the early universe.
  • Stellar black holes: Formed from the collapse of massive stars.
  • Supermassive black holes: Found at the centers of galaxies, like the black hole at the center of the Milky Way.
    •

    Interesting facts:

  • A black hole cannot be seen directly, but its effects on nearby stars and materials can be observed.
  • The first image of a black hole was captured in 2019, showing a bright ring around a dark area.
  • Black holes do not "suck" things as commonly believed; rather, objects fall into them due to gravity.
